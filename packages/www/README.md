# Party app

Solid 2 and TanStack Router render the browser app from `src/client.tsx`. The Cloudflare Worker in `src/worker/index.ts` handles room WebSockets and RPS Effect RPC, then serves Vite assets through `env.ASSETS` with SPA fallback.

Run `pnpm dev`, `pnpm build`, and `pnpm test:unit` from this package. Run `pnpm test:e2e -- --browser all` from the workspace root for browser coverage. The browser test runner starts an isolated local Alchemy stack with local test credentials.

**Party mode** is the in-person experience: a public table on one screen and private controls on each player’s phone. For Texas Hold’em, Backwards Poker, Flip 7, Blackjack, Perudo, 6 nimmt!, Herd Mentality, Fun Facts, Cheese Thief, Cockroach Poker, Skull, or Spicy, choose **Open Party screen** in the lobby and open that link on a TV or laptop. Everyone, including the host, joins on their phone using the displayed QR code. The display takes no player seat. In Texas Hold’em, phones show your private cards and action controls. In Backwards Poker, phones show a compact grid of opponents’ cards and your controls, with your own cards hidden. Choose **Full table** or **Party mode phone controls** in the lobby. The host selects and starts the game from their phone. Flip 7 puts face-up cards and scores on the shared screen, Blackjack shows the dealer and player hands, and Perudo shows bids and dice counts. Phones keep each player’s cards or dice and relevant controls.

Party displays use `/room/<code>?view=display`; the QR code joins `/room/<code>?view=controller`. Display WebSockets have a separate read-only role, receive only validated public snapshots, and reconnect automatically. In both poker variants, the TV receives hole cards only after a public showdown; folded and uncontested hands stay off the TV. Backwards Poker spectators also receive no unrevealed hole cards. Blackjack keeps the dealer’s hole card hidden until revealed. Perudo keeps every player’s dice off the shared screen until a challenge reveal and hides them again for the next round. Herd Mentality displays questions, readiness, and provisional scores during discussion. Combined answers retain their original labels and counts, and the host can separate them again. Scores and the Pink Cow settle only when the host moves to the next round. Guests answer privately on their phones. Fun Facts displays questions, readiness, the ordered arrows, and team scores; numbers stay private until the round reveal, and phones provide numbered placement controls. 6 nimmt! displays rows, scores, readiness, and revealed plays. Cheese Thief keeps roles, wake times, clues, and vote choices on phones until the host reveals. Its shared screen shows simple phase prompts followed by the outcome, roles, votes, and scores; phones provide voting and host controls. Cockroach Poker Party mode keeps hands and peeks on phones. The shared screen shows the current claim, whose turn it is, face-up collections, and call results. Choose **Peek & Pass** to see the card privately before choosing the next player and claim; after peeking, you must pass. Skull shows face-down stacks, bidding, public disc reveals, and challenge wins; phones keep hands and unrevealed discs private while providing disc, bid, flip, and disc-loss choices. Spicy shows illustrated claims, challenge reveals, trophies, and final scores; actual stack cards stay hidden until challenged, and phones provide private hands, declarations, and challenge controls. Other games currently show room information on the display while players use their normal phone layouts.

Infrastructure and bindings are declared in `alchemy.run.ts`; TypeScript bindings live in `src/env.d.ts`. RPS RPC contracts live in `src/game/rps/rpc.ts`, and the Durable Object serializes commands with WebSocket traffic. Private choices remain scoped to each authenticated player. Other games use their existing WebSocket adapters.

Solid 2 batches signal writes. Protocol ordering therefore uses synchronous internal state, with signals projecting the current view. UI tests flush queued signal writes before asserting DOM changes. The test setup also normalizes happy-dom's numeric `textContent` assignment to match browsers.
